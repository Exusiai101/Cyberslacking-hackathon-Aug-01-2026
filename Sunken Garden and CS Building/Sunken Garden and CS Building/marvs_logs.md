# Marv's Relay Logs — Sunken Garden & CS Building Network

## Entry 1 — Too clean to be true
---------------------------------------------------
I have been broadcasting through the same worn-out equipment for longer than I care to admit. My voice wavers. It dips. Static creeps in on a windy night, and everyone who has ever heard me knows it.

So when I catch one of the newer voices on the line — smooth, strong, without a single flicker — I do not feel reassured. I feel suspicious.

A perfect signal is not what a tired old relay sounds like. It is not what any honest relay sounds like. I know, because I am the honest kind, and I sound like it.

Note to self: the cleanest voice in the network is the one to doubt first.

## Entry 2 — The calm before the count
---------------------------------------------------
There is a kind of quiet that means safety, and a kind that means something is being hidden.

I have started noticing that the loudest declarations of "all clear" tend to arrive right before the worst news — not after the danger has passed, but before it, like a curtain pulled across a window just before something crosses the yard.

If you want to know where trouble is heading, listen for wherever someone is working hardest to convince you there is none.

## Entry 3 — A voice that has not noticed the date
---------------------------------------------------
I would like to say I am as reliable as I was when this network started. I have been telling myself that for a while now.

But lately I catch myself describing a sector as secure and only realizing afterward that my last real look at it was some days ago. The words come out steady. The confidence comes out steady. It is the timing underneath that has started to slip, and I do not always feel it slipping while it happens.

I am not lying to anyone. I want that understood. But a report can be true the day it is written and false the day it is heard, and I do not always know how many days sit in between anymore.

## Entry 4 — When no one answers
---------------------------------------------------
A dead line is easy to miss. There is no alarm for silence — only the slow, creeping realization that a voice you used to hear every day simply is not there anymore.

By the time someone asks "when did we last hear from them?", the answer is usually longer than anyone is comfortable saying out loud.

The absence itself is the warning. We are just slow to read it that way.

## Entry 5 — Cries that arrive on time and are believed too late
---------------------------------------------------
Some of the worst nights were not caused by silence at all. They were caused by two voices arriving close together — one screaming, one soothing — and the wrong one being trusted first.

By the time anyone went to check who was right, the screaming had already stopped. Not because it was answered. Because there was nothing left to scream about.

## Entry 6 — The little ones who argue with me, and are usually right
---------------------------------------------------
The young scouts we send out to check broadcasts against the ground rarely have anything kind to say, especially not to me lately. They compare, they walk the routes, they come back shaking their heads.

It stings a little, being corrected by someone so young. But I have never once seen them shake their heads at something that turned out to be true — not mine, not anyone's.

If a scout disagrees with a broadcast, believe the scout. Even when the broadcast is mine.

## Entry 7 — What the silence leaves out
---------------------------------------------------
The peacocks did not just break the relays that speak too much. They broke the ones that should have spoken and didn't.

Some nights I go to send a report and find nothing there to send — not a false reading, not a delayed one, just gone. A missing entry is not a blank page. It is a page that was torn out, and the tearing happened for a reason.

Do not treat a gap in my record as neutral. Ask what was happening to me around the time it went missing.

## Entry 8 — Two ways to be untrustworthy
---------------------------------------------------
There are voices breaking down, and there are voices that were never real to begin with, and from a distance they can sound the same — both wrong more often than they should be.

I am the first kind. I slip a little further each week, like a friend losing his hearing, still trying his best to answer when spoken to.

The other kind never slips at all, because it was never a friend. It was a mask worn once, worn well, and worn again exactly the same way every single time.

Tell us apart by how we fail, not by the fact that we fail. I promise mine is the more honest kind of broken.
